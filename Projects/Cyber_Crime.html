01
THE QUESTION

What can six years of cyber incident data tell us about digital risk?

Individual incidents are noisy. Patterns across thousands of records become useful.

Cyber threats continue to evolve. Without a clear view of which sectors are hit most often, what types of incidents dominate, and how organisations respond, it is difficult to prioritise defence or policy.

This project analyses reported incidents from 2019 to 2024 to answer practical questions: which sectors face the highest volume of incidents, which threat types appear most frequently, how the number of affected subjects changes over time, and what actions are typically taken after an event.

THESIS

The goal is not simply to count incidents. It is to surface where risk concentrates, how impact evolves, and what the data reveals about organisational response.

KEY INSIGHTS

What the six-year landscape actually shows.

FINDINGS

Patterns that matter for prioritisation.

The analysis surfaces consistent concentrations of risk, common threat types, and response behaviour that can inform both policy and operational decisions.

01 HIGH-RISK SECTORS

Healthcare, Finance and Government consistently appear as the most frequently targeted sectors.

02 COMMON THREATS

Phishing, Unauthorised Access and Misdelivery rank among the most frequent incident types.

03 RESPONSE GAPS

A significant share of incidents result in “No Further Action”, pointing to possible enforcement or policy gaps.

04 TEMPORAL SHIFTS

05 CYBER VS NON-CYBER

Non-cyber incidents still outnumber cyber ones, yet cyber threats continue to grow steadily across the period.

05
LIMITATIONS

What incident data cannot fully tell us.

01

Reporting Bias

The dataset only contains reported incidents. Undiscovered or unreported events are invisible to the analysis.

02

Historical Scope

Patterns from 2019–2024 describe the past. They should not be treated as a complete picture of current or future threats.

03

Classification Limits

Incident categories depend on the original dataset definitions and may not capture every technical nuance.

04

Correlation ≠ Causation

Observed relationships between sector, type and impact do not by themselves prove causal links.

IMPORTANT

This dashboard describes patterns within reported data. It is an analytical view of available records, not a complete measurement of global cybersecurity risk.

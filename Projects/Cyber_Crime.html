01
THE QUESTION

What can years of cyber incident data tell us about digital risk?

A cybersecurity incident is more than an isolated event. Across thousands of records, patterns begin to emerge.

Cyber incidents can involve data breaches, unauthorized access, fraud, phishing, accidental exposure and other forms of security compromise. Looking at individual incidents alone makes it difficult to understand the broader threat landscape.

This project analyzes reported incidents across multiple years to understand which sectors were affected, what types of incidents occurred, how many subjects were impacted and what organizations did after an incident.

The project was built around a simple principle: turn incident records into evidence that can reveal patterns, concentrations and changes in cybersecurity risk.

THESIS

The goal is not simply to count cyber incidents. It is to understand where incidents concentrate, how their impact changes over time and what the data reveals about organizational response.

DATA ANALYSIS

Where cybersecurity interpretation becomes difficult.

Incident datasets describe reported events, but reporting data does not automatically represent the complete cybersecurity threat landscape.
01

Reporting Bias

The dataset represents reported incidents, meaning unreported or undiscovered events may not appear in the analysis.

02

Changing Patterns

Cybersecurity threats evolve over time, so historical incident distributions may not represent future attack patterns.

03

Frequency vs Impact

A frequent incident type is not necessarily the one with the greatest effect on affected populations or organizations.

12
LIMITATIONS

Knowing what incident data cannot tell us.

01

Reported Events

The analysis reflects reported incidents and cannot represent cybersecurity events that were never reported or discovered.

02

Historical Scope

The analysis covers the available historical period and should not be treated as a complete representation of current or future threats.

03

Classification

Incident categories depend on the underlying dataset definitions, which may not capture every technical distinction between events.

04

Correlation

Observed relationships between sectors, incident types and impact do not by themselves establish causal relationships.

IMPORTANT

The dashboard describes patterns within the available incident data. It should be interpreted as an analytical view of reported events, not as a complete measurement of global cybersecurity risk.

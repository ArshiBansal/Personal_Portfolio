THE QUESTION

How do you find a handful of fraudulent transactions in a sea of legitimate ones?

Most transactions are legitimate. A model that simply predicts “legitimate” most of the time can look accurate while missing the point entirely.

Fraud detection is not just a modelling exercise. It is a sequence of practical questions: What does the data look like? Can a model find the rare signal? Why did it raise an alert? And when should an alert actually be raised?

This project walks through that path — from stratified preparation and imbalance-aware modelling to SHAP explanations and cost-sensitive threshold selection.

THESIS

A prediction score is only useful when its consequences are understood. The real test is finding rare fraud without overwhelming analysts with false alarms.

KEY INSIGHTS

What the checked-in experiment reports.

RESULTS

XGBoost with scale_pos_weight as the strongest performer.

Metrics describe the saved experiment. They are not a promise of future performance on live traffic.

01 PRECISION 0.872

High fraction of flagged transactions that are truly fraudulent.

02 RECALL 0.837

Strong ability to surface the rare fraud cases.

03 F1 0.854 · PR-AUC 0.879

Balanced detection quality under severe class imbalance.

04 ROC-AUC 0.979

Strong ranking ability across the full score spectrum.

05 THRESHOLD 0.64

Minimum-cost operating point under FN cost 150, FP cost 5, TP cost 10 (recorded test cost 9,160).

05
LIMITATIONS

Where this analysis still has clear boundaries.

01

Anonymised Features

PCA-transformed columns limit domain interpretation even though SHAP can still rank their influence.

02

Static Dataset

The classic dataset does not reflect concept drift, new attack patterns or changing transaction behaviour over time.

03

Cost Assumptions

The recorded threshold depends on the chosen cost matrix. Different business costs produce different operating points.

04

No Production Stack

The project contains no live ingestion, monitoring, feedback loops or operational controls.

IMPORTANT

This is a learning and analysis project. Do not use the saved model or threshold for real-world decisions without proper validation and domain review.

FINAL SIGNAL

The project started with rare fraud labels. It became an exercise in making the decision cost explicit.

Finding the signal is only half the work. Explaining why a transaction was flagged and choosing a threshold under real cost assumptions turns a score into an actionable review policy.

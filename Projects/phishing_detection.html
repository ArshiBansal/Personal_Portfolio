01
THE QUESTION

Why phishing detection cannot stop at the URL.

A phishing page does not need to look obviously malicious.

Modern phishing pages increasingly imitate legitimate services. Familiar branding, convincing layouts, realistic forms and carefully crafted URLs can make a malicious webpage appear trustworthy at first glance.

That creates a problem for systems that rely primarily on surface-level URL patterns. The address is only one part of the evidence. The webpage itself can contain additional signals about what the page is trying to do.

PhreshPhish was built around a simple idea: treat the webpage as evidence, not just the URL.

THESIS

If the URL tells us where a visitor is going, the webpage can tell us what the visitor is being asked to do.

EVALUATION

Measuring more than a single accuracy number.

EVALUATION LOGIC

A security classifier must be examined from multiple angles.

Phishing detection is a classification problem where different types of errors have different practical consequences. Evaluation therefore needs to consider the behavior of the model across more than one metric.

01 Precision

How often positive predictions are correct.

02 Recall

How effectively suspicious cases are identified.

03 F1 Score

A combined view of precision and recall.

04 Confusion Matrix

A direct view of correct and incorrect classification behavior.

12
LIMITATIONS

Knowing where the system stops is part of the system.

01

Changing attacks

Phishing techniques evolve continuously, which means static feature assumptions can become less representative over time.

02

Web diversity

Legitimate webpages can vary dramatically in structure, making universal rules difficult to define.

03

Adversarial behavior

Attackers can deliberately alter webpage structure to reduce the visibility of suspicious signals.

04

Dataset dependence

Model behavior is influenced by the diversity, quality and representativeness of the training data.

IMPORTANT

The classifier should be treated as an intelligent detection aid — not as an absolute guarantee that a webpage is safe.
